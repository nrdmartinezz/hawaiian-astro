## 2. Treatment-specific FAQ answers

### A. General orthodontic treatment

#### 1. What orthodontic problems can Hawaiian Smiles Orthodontics treat?
The website names overbites, underbites, crossbites, crowded teeth, spacing problems, protruding teeth, open bites, and midline concerns. The exact problems treated and the recommended approach depend on the patient’s examination. [1] [11]

#### 2. How does an orthodontist decide whether a child, teen, or adult needs treatment?
The orthodontist reviews the patient’s dental and medical history, performs an oral exam, takes X-rays, and evaluates tooth and jaw development. The team then creates a treatment plan based on the patient’s needs and goals. [7]

#### 3. What happens during the examination and X-rays used to create a treatment plan?
The practice says it reviews medical and dental history and provides a complete oral exam with X-rays. These records help the team determine whether treatment is needed and develop a customized plan. [7]

#### 4. How long might orthodontic treatment take for my individual bite and alignment needs?
The website does not give one timeline for every patient. It says treatment length depends on the severity of the problem, the appliance selected, and the length and complexity of the personalized plan. [1] [3]

#### 5. What factors can change the length of an orthodontic treatment plan?
The practice identifies problem severity, treatment device, treatment complexity, and patient-specific needs as factors. Wearing aligners as directed and following care instructions may also affect progress, but the orthodontist should explain the details for each case. [1] [3] [16]

#### 6. How often will I need appointments during treatment?
Appointment timing varies by treatment. The homepage says traditional braces appointments are often every four to eight weeks. Invisalign and self-ligating systems may require fewer or more widely spaced visits, but the office should provide the patient’s schedule. [1] [13] [16]

#### 7. What can I do if my teeth feel sore after an appointment?
The practice says some soreness or discomfort is common when an appliance is first placed or adjusted. Patients should follow the office’s care instructions and contact the practice if pain is severe, unmanageable, or accompanied by swelling or infection. [14]

#### 8. How should I brush and floss while wearing braces or aligners?
For braces, the website recommends careful hygiene and brushing after meals, with an interdental brush or floss threader to clean around appliances. For aligners, remove them for brushing and flossing, then clean the aligners as instructed. [16] [17] [18]

#### 9. Can I play sports or an instrument during orthodontic treatment?
The reviewed pages do not give a complete sports or instrument policy. The emergency page recommends a mouthguard for contact sports. Ask the orthodontist how to protect a specific appliance during sports or music activities. [14]

#### 10. What should I do if an orthodontic appliance breaks or begins to bother my mouth?
Contact the office for instructions. The emergency page lists loose or broken brackets, bands, wires, and poking wires as common orthodontic problems. Orthodontic wax may provide temporary protection for an irritated area while the patient arranges care. [14]

#### 11. Will I need retainers after braces or clear aligner treatment?
The practice describes retention as an important part of treatment after braces or Invisalign. Retainers help hold teeth in their new positions, and the orthodontist will provide a wear plan. [15]

#### 12. How will the orthodontist decide which treatment option fits my goals and oral health?
The orthodontist evaluates the patient’s teeth, bite, jaw development, oral health, goals, and lifestyle. The first visit includes an exam, X-rays, and a discussion of treatment options before a customized plan is recommended. [7]

### B. Early treatment and Phase 1 care

#### 1. When should a child have an orthodontic evaluation?
The early-treatment page cites the American Association of Orthodontists’ guidance that children should have an orthodontic visit by age seven. An evaluation does not necessarily mean treatment will start immediately. [11]

#### 2. What is early orthodontic treatment, and how is it different from full treatment later?
Early treatment guides dental and jaw development while a child still has a mixture of primary and permanent teeth. It may address developing problems before the permanent teeth fully erupt. A later phase may still be needed, depending on the child’s growth and treatment needs. [11]

#### 3. Does an early evaluation always mean that a child needs treatment right away?
No. The practice says an evaluation may show that treatment is not needed immediately. It can help the orthodontist monitor development and identify signs of future problems. [11]

#### 4. What dental or jaw concerns may lead Hawaiian Smiles to recommend Phase 1 treatment?
The site discusses severe crossbite, crowding, protruding teeth, severe underbite, narrow arches, and problems related to thumb sucking, tongue thrusting, or mouth breathing. The orthodontist must decide whether treatment is appropriate for the individual child. [11] [5]

#### 5. How can early treatment help with crowding, crossbites, protruding teeth, or severe underbites?
The practice says early treatment may guide growth, create space for permanent teeth, correct certain bite problems, and make later treatment more efficient. It does not guarantee that surgery, extractions, or later treatment will be avoided. [11]

#### 6. How can a palatal expander help a child with a narrow upper jaw or crossbite?
The practice says a palatal expander gradually widens the upper jaw and may help correct a severe crossbite while the jaw is developing. The orthodontist decides whether expansion is needed and which appliance is suitable. [11] [5]

#### 7. Can early treatment address habits such as thumb sucking, tongue thrusting, or mouth breathing?
The early-treatment page discusses these habits as behaviors that may affect tooth and jaw development. Hawaiian Smiles may evaluate their orthodontic effects and recommend care when appropriate. The website does not promise that orthodontics alone will resolve every breathing or habit-related concern. [11]

#### 8. How long might a child wait between Phase 1 treatment and a later treatment phase?
The reviewed pages do not give one waiting period. Timing depends on dental development, growth, the first treatment’s goals, and the orthodontist’s follow-up findings. [11]

#### 9. Will a child who receives early treatment still need braces or aligners later?
Possibly. The practice explains that early treatment can make a later phase more efficient, but it does not say that every child will or will not need additional treatment. The orthodontist should explain the likely stages for each child. [5] [11]

#### 10. How can parents help a child care for an orthodontic appliance?
Parents can help the child follow wear instructions, brush and floss carefully, avoid foods that can damage the appliance, keep removable appliances in their case, and contact the office if an appliance is lost or damaged. [5] [11] [15]

### C. Invisalign First and palatal expansion

#### 1. What is Invisalign First, and which children may be evaluated for it?
Invisalign First is a clear-aligner treatment designed for developing smiles and used as a Phase 1 option. The practice says it may be considered for younger children, often around ages six to ten, but candidacy depends on the child’s examination. [5]

#### 2. How is Invisalign First used as a Phase 1 treatment for children around ages six to ten?
The practice says Invisalign First can guide developing teeth, address certain bite problems, create room for permanent teeth, and make a later phase more efficient. The age range is a general description, not a guarantee of candidacy. [5]

#### 3. Can Invisalign First help with spacing, crowding, narrow arches, or developing bite problems?
The website says Invisalign First is designed to address spacing, crowding, narrow arches, and other developing orthodontic issues. The orthodontist must determine whether it is appropriate for a child’s specific condition. [5]

#### 4. How does Invisalign First compare with traditional braces for a young child?
The practice describes Invisalign First as removable and free of brackets and wires, which may make brushing and flossing easier. It still requires consistent wear and may not be appropriate for every child. [5]

#### 5. How does the Invisalign First Palatal Expander System widen the upper arch?
The site says a digital scan is used to create custom expanders, each stage gradually widens the upper arch, and small tooth-colored attachments help keep the expander secure. A holding phase may follow expansion. [5]

#### 6. What happens during the digital scan used to create Invisalign First expanders?
The practice uses a digital scan to create a model of the child’s mouth and design a series of custom expanders. The team should explain the scan and treatment sequence during the consultation. [5]

#### 7. How much time each day does a child need to wear Invisalign First?
The Invisalign First page says full-time wear is important and that the appliance should be removed only for brushing and flossing. The orthodontist should give the child’s exact wear instructions. [5]

#### 8. Can a child eat while wearing an Invisalign First palatal expander?
The page says children can eat with the Invisalign palatal expander in place, but should avoid sticky or chewy foods. Follow the practice’s specific instructions for the child’s appliance. [5]

#### 9. How should a child clean Invisalign First aligners or expanders?
The practice recommends a soft toothbrush and cool water and says not to use hot water. Children should also brush and floss their teeth as directed. [5]

#### 10. Are temporary pressure or speech changes normal when a child begins palate expansion?
The practice says light pressure and temporary speech changes can occur at first. It also says a small space between the front teeth may appear during expansion and that many children adapt within a few days. Contact the office about severe or persistent symptoms. [5]

#### 11. What should parents do if a child loses or damages an Invisalign First appliance?
Contact Hawaiian Smiles promptly for instructions. The office needs to decide whether the child should use a prior appliance, move to another stage, or receive a replacement. [5]

#### 12. How does the orthodontist decide whether Invisalign First is appropriate for a child?
The orthodontist reviews the child’s dental development, bite, available space, habits, jaw width, oral health, and ability to follow wear and care instructions. The practice makes the final recommendation after an evaluation. [5] [7]

### D. Braces and Pitts21 self-ligating brackets

#### 1. How do traditional braces move teeth and improve a bite?
The braces page says brackets and archwires apply directed forces to move teeth toward better positions. The orthodontist controls the treatment through periodic visits and adjustments. [18]

#### 2. What is the difference between traditional metal braces and ceramic braces?
The practice describes traditional metal braces as durable stainless-steel appliances. It also lists ceramic braces as a more discreet option. The orthodontist can explain which material fits the patient’s needs. [1] [18]

#### 3. What are Pitts21 self-ligating brackets?
Pitts21 brackets are a self-ligating brace system used by Hawaiian Smiles. Instead of elastic ties, the brackets use small doors to hold the archwire. The practice describes the system as aesthetic and designed for three-dimensional control. [13]

#### 4. How do self-ligating brackets differ from braces that use elastic ties?
Self-ligating brackets use built-in doors rather than elastic ties to hold the wire. The practice says this can make brackets easier to keep clean and appointments shorter because elastic ties do not need to be changed. [13]

#### 5. What does the practice mean when it describes Pitts21 brackets as an aesthetic self-ligating system?
The practice says the brackets are designed with rounded corners and a placement approach intended to support the natural curve of the smile. These are the practice’s technology and treatment claims; the orthodontist should explain expected results for an individual case. [13]

#### 6. Could self-ligating brackets mean shorter or less frequent appointments for some patients?
The practice says visits may be shorter and appointments may be spaced farther apart than with conventional treatment. The exact schedule depends on the treatment plan. [13]

#### 7. How long might treatment with self-ligating brackets take for my case?
The self-ligating-brackets page gives a general range of about 12 to 30 months. The practice says actual time depends on individual orthodontic needs, so the range is not a personal treatment estimate. [13]

#### 8. Do self-ligating brackets require tightening or other regular adjustments?
The page says its self-ligating brackets do not require traditional tightening and that appointments may be spaced farther apart. Patients still need regular visits so the orthodontist can monitor progress and make any needed changes. [13]

#### 9. What foods should I avoid while wearing braces?
The practice recommends avoiding hard foods such as popcorn and hard taco shells, crunchy foods such as apples, and sticky foods such as licorice. Follow the office’s current food list. [18]

#### 10. How can I clean around brackets and wires after meals?
The braces page recommends brushing after every meal and considering an interdental brush or floss threader to remove food around the braces. [18]

#### 11. What should I do if a bracket comes loose or a wire begins to poke?
Contact the orthodontic office. The emergency page says loose or broken brackets and poking wires are common orthodontic problems; wax can provide temporary protection from irritation. [14]

#### 12. How can I protect my braces while playing contact sports?
The emergency page recommends using a mouthguard for contact sports. Ask the practice which type is suitable for the specific appliance. [14]

#### 13. What level of soreness or pressure is common after braces are placed or adjusted?
The practice says soreness or discomfort is common when an appliance is first placed or adjusted, but it should not be severe. Contact the office for severe, unmanageable, or worsening pain. [14]

#### 14. How does the orthodontist decide whether braces or clear aligners are a better fit?
The orthodontist considers the patient’s bite, tooth movements needed, oral health, lifestyle, goals, and ability to follow removable-aligner instructions. The first visit includes an exam, X-rays, and a customized treatment discussion. [7] [16]

### E. Invisalign Adult and Invisalign Teen

#### 1. How does Invisalign clear aligner treatment work?
Invisalign uses a series of custom clear trays. Each tray is designed for a stage of tooth movement, and the patient changes trays according to the orthodontist’s instructions. [16]

#### 2. How does the practice use digital scans and 3D planning for Invisalign treatment?
The practice says it uses a scan or other records to create a 3D model of the mouth and plan custom aligners. The digital plan may also show an estimate of how the smile could look after treatment. [16] [17]

#### 3. How many hours each day should an adult or teen wear Invisalign aligners?
The adult Invisalign page says about 22 hours per day. The teen page says 20 to 22 hours per day. The orthodontist should give the exact wear schedule for the patient’s plan. [16] [17]

#### 4. How often do patients usually change to the next Invisalign aligner?
The practice says adult patients generally wear each set for about two weeks, while the homepage describes one- to two-week changes. Follow the schedule provided by the orthodontist rather than changing trays early. [1] [16]

#### 5. Can I remove Invisalign aligners to eat, drink, brush, and floss?
Yes. The practice says aligners are removable for eating, drinking, brushing, and flossing. Patients should put them back in as directed and keep them in when they are not eating, drinking something other than plain water, or cleaning their teeth. [16] [17]

#### 6. What can I drink while wearing Invisalign aligners?
The teen page says patients should remove aligners for anything other than plain water. Food and drinks with aligners may damage or discolor them. [17]

#### 7. How should I clean Invisalign aligners without damaging them?
The teen page recommends a soft-bristled toothbrush, liquid soap, and lukewarm water. The Invisalign First page emphasizes cool water and avoiding hot water. Follow the practice’s appliance-specific instructions. [5] [17]

#### 8. Is Invisalign appropriate for every orthodontic problem, or does candidacy depend on the case?
Candidacy depends on the patient’s condition. The practice says Invisalign can treat many patients, including some complex cases, but an evaluation is needed to determine whether it is suitable. [3] [17]

#### 9. How does Invisalign Teen differ from adult Invisalign treatment?
The practice says Invisalign Teen includes compliance indicators and that teens may receive replacement aligners if one is misplaced. The wear, cleaning, and clinical requirements still depend on the individual treatment plan. [17]

#### 10. What are Invisalign Teen compliance indicators, and how do they help track wear?
The practice says the indicators change color to help show whether the aligners have been worn for the required amount of time. They support monitoring but do not replace regular appointments or the patient’s responsibility to follow instructions. [17]

#### 11. What should a teen do if an Invisalign aligner is lost or damaged?
The teen should contact the orthodontist promptly. The practice says some teen patients may have replacement aligners, but the office should decide what to wear next so treatment does not lose progress. [17]

#### 12. Can Invisalign fit around school, work, travel, sports, or playing an instrument?
The practice presents Invisalign as removable and discreet, with fewer visits in some cases. Patients still need to wear the aligners for the required hours and should ask the orthodontist how to manage them during specific activities. [16] [17]

#### 13. Does Invisalign usually require fewer office visits than braces?
The practice says Invisalign may require fewer follow-up appointments because there are no wires to adjust. The actual schedule varies by treatment plan and compliance. [16] [17]

#### 14. How does the orthodontist compare Invisalign with braces for treatment time and results?
The orthodontist compares the patient’s clinical needs, required tooth movements, goals, lifestyle, and ability to wear aligners consistently. The website says Invisalign can be efficient and effective, but it does not promise the same timeline or result for every patient. [16] [17]

#### 15. What happens if a patient does not wear Invisalign for the recommended number of hours?
Because aligners work only while they are worn, missed wear can affect progress and may require instructions from the orthodontist. Teens should contact the office if they repeatedly cannot meet the recommended schedule. [17]

#### 16. How can parents support a teen who is responsible for removable aligners?
Parents can help the teen keep the aligner case available, follow the wear schedule, remove aligners for meals, clean them properly, and contact the office quickly if an aligner is lost or damaged. [17]

#### 7. How might an adult’s treatment time differ from a teen’s treatment time?

#### 8. Can adult patients request a more discreet treatment option?

#### 9. What should adults know about soreness, speech, eating, and cleaning during treatment?

#### 10. What will the orthodontist review before recommending a treatment plan for an adult?
The team reviews medical and dental history, performs an exam with X-rays, discusses goals and treatment options, and reviews financial and insurance information. [7]

### G. Emergencies and retention

#### 1. What counts as an orthodontic emergency at Hawaiian Smiles?
The practice lists trauma or injury, infection or swelling, severe unmanageable pain, and problems such as loose or broken brackets, bands, or wires. The level of urgency depends on the situation. [14]

#### 2. When should I go to an emergency room instead of waiting for an orthodontic appointment?
The emergency page says to seek emergency care first for trauma, infection or swelling, severe pain, uncontrolled bleeding, or another condition that could endanger the patient. Contact the orthodontist after the urgent situation is stabilized. [14]

#### 3. What should I do about a loose or broken bracket, band, or wire?
Contact the practice to arrange advice or an appointment. A loose bracket or wire is often an orthodontic problem rather than a life-threatening emergency, but the office should determine how quickly it needs attention. [14]

#### 4. How can orthodontic wax help with a poking wire or bracket while I wait for care?
Wax can cover the irritating edge and reduce rubbing against the cheek or lip. It is a temporary measure; contact the office for a lasting repair. [14]

#### 5. What should I do if I have swelling, severe pain, an infection, or uncontrolled bleeding?
Seek urgent medical or dental care, including an emergency room when appropriate. Then notify the orthodontic office so the team can help address orthodontic issues after the immediate danger is managed. [14]

#### 6. What should I do if I lose an Invisalign aligner during treatment?
Contact the orthodontist promptly. The practice says a lost aligner can interrupt progress and that some teen patients may have replacement trays, but the office must tell the patient what to wear next. [17]

#### 7. Why are retainers important after braces or Invisalign treatment?
Retainers help hold teeth in their new positions. The practice says tooth position can change over time without continued retainer care. [15]

#### 8. How long might I need to wear my retainer full time?
The retention page says full-time wear commonly lasts about two years, but the schedule varies by patient. The orthodontist should provide the patient’s exact instructions. [15]

#### 9. Why might the practice recommend part-time retainer wear for the long term?
The practice explains that teeth can shift over time, even after treatment. It often recommends part-time retainer wear indefinitely to help maintain alignment. [15]

#### 10. How often are retainer follow-up appointments scheduled after treatment?
The practice says follow-up appointments during the full-time retention period are scheduled every three to six months. The schedule may change based on the patient’s progress. [15]

#### 11. How should I clean and store my retainer?
The practice recommends brushing the retainer with soap and cool water, occasionally soaking it in dental cleanser, keeping it in its case when it is not being worn, and keeping it away from pets. Do not use hot water. [15]

#### 12. What should I do if my retainer is lost, broken, or no longer fits?
Contact the practice right away. The retention page says a lost or broken retainer can affect the new smile and may require repair or replacement. [15]

#### 13. Can teeth shift after orthodontic treatment even when the braces have been removed?
Yes. The practice says tooth position commonly changes over time and that retainers are needed to help maintain the result. [15]

#### 14. Are retainer repairs or replacements included in my treatment fee, or are they billed separately?
The reviewed pages do not say whether repairs or replacements are included. Ask the financial coordinator to confirm the current agreement before treatment begins. [8] [15]

## Editorial and approval notes

- **Confirm current facts:** The website’s homepage and older pages contain slightly different contact, location, and provider wording. Verify the current office list, map links, phone and text numbers, doctor roster, and hours.
- **Avoid guarantees:** Do not promise a particular result, comfort level, treatment time, insurance payment, or avoidance of extractions or surgery.
- **Use patient-specific language:** Say “may,” “can,” or “the orthodontist will evaluate” rather than presenting treatment as appropriate for everyone.
- **Confirm money details:** Published fees, monthly-payment examples, discounts, deposits, and insurance estimates should be reviewed by the practice before publication.
- **Emergency language:** Keep the emergency-room direction clear for trauma, uncontrolled bleeding, severe swelling, infection, and severe or unmanageable pain.
