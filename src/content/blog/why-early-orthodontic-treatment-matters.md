The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first check by age 7. At that point, enough adult teeth are in for Dr. Nayak, Dr. Shaikh, and Dr. Nicole to see how the jaws are growing — and to catch problems while they are still easy to steer.

## What Phase 1 actually does

Early treatment is not about putting every seven-year-old in braces. It is a short, focused plan for issues that get harder as the face finishes growing:

- Crossbites that shift the jaw off center
- Crowding that leaves no path for adult teeth
- Thumb-sucking or mouth-breathing habits that change the palate
- Protruding front teeth that are easier to injure on the playground

A typical Phase 1 plan lasts 9–15 months, then we pause and watch while the rest of the adult teeth come in. Phase 2 — full braces or Invisalign — is often shorter and simpler because the foundation is already set.

## What a first visit looks like

Bring your child to any of our offices. We take photos and a gentle scan, talk through what we see, and only recommend starting if waiting would make treatment harder. Many kids leave with a “see you in a year” plan and no appliance at all.
