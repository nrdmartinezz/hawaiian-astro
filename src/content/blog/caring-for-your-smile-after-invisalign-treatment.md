Finishing Invisalign is the end of the trays, not the end of the work. The teeth are in a new place, and they will drift if nothing holds them. Here is what [Dr. Satya Nayak, Dr. Alyssa Shaikh, and Dr. Nicole Fernandez](/about/doctors/) ask patients to do once the last aligner comes out.

## Wear the retainer the way we prescribed it

Almost everyone leaves with a retainer. Yours is based on your bite, not a generic schedule. [Full-time wear comes first](/treatments/retainers/), often for about two years, with checkups every few months. After that, nights — and for many people, nights for the long run.

Skip the hard, crunchy foods for the first couple of weeks while the teeth settle. Younger patients usually keep a retainer through the years when wisdom teeth erupt or are removed. If a retainer cracks or disappears, call [(808) 247-6039](tel:+18082476039) the same day. The longer you wait, the more the teeth move.

## Brushing and flossing without trays in the way

Aligners covered the teeth for months. Once they are off, plaque has a clear path to the gumline. Brush twice a day and floss once. Plaque that stays becomes tartar, and tartar is what leads to gingivitis.

Gums can feel tender for a week or two. A warm salt-water rinse helps. Teeth can feel sensitive too, because they are no longer covered by plastic all day. A sensitivity toothpaste is enough for most people. If something still hurts at your next visit, tell us. Staining that showed up during treatment is a separate question. We can talk about [whitening](/treatments/teeth-whitening/) once the retainer fit is stable.

## Keep seeing your dentist

Orthodontic visits do not replace cleanings and exams. Your dentist is the one who finds cavities and checks the gums while we watch the bite. If a cavity shows up, treating it early is simpler than treating it after the teeth have shifted around it.
