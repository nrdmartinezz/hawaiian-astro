## What Is Tongue Thrusting?

Tongue thrusting, also known as reverse swallowing or immature swallowing, is a muscle imbalance that causes the tongue to press too far forward in the mouth during the speech, swallowing, and even rest.

Tongue thrusting is regular in newborns, but if tongue thrusting continues after the age of four and into adulthood, it can lead to orthodontic problems, including an open bite (when the front teeth do not meet when the mouth is closed). Early detection and treatment of tongue-thrust diseases can assist in avoiding more serious problems from occurring.

## Tongue Thrust-Related Problems

A person will swallow between 1,200 and 2,000 times per day on average. The tongue exerts between 1 and 6 pounds of pressure on the teeth and other oral structures during each swallow. After a while, this constant pressure on the teeth might result in tooth misalignment and biting problems.

The pressure from your tongue pressing on the rear of your teeth can cause your front teeth to migrate outward. This results in a space between your center top and bottom teeth, known as an open bite.

Untreated tongue thrust can also cause long-term speech impairment, such as a lisp over specific sounds. It can also cause your tongue to protrude between your teeth, elongating your face.

## How To Stop Tongue Thrust?

Orthodontic devices, in general, can provide effective treatment for tongue thrusting. Work with your orthodontist to determine the best treatment option for you. Orofacial myology is sometimes indicated as a treatment. This is a treatment that corrects the position of the lips, jaw, and tongue position over time. Swallowing habits are also addressed in this therapy.

Open bite corrections done without continuous therapy have been known to revert over time. Any sinus, allergy, or breathing difficulties that may be causing your or your child's tongue pushing should be addressed by your doctor. For swallowing therapy to be successful, breathing concerns must be addressed.

You or your child may need speech therapy in addition to swallowing therapy to correct any speech difficulties that have formed as a result of tongue thrusting.
